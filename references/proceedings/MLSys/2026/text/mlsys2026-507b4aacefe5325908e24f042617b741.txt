                                                         A BSTRACT
        Tensor parallelism (TP) enables large language models (LLMs) to scale inference efficiently across multiple GPUs,
        but its tight coupling makes systems fragile: a single GPU failure can halt execution, trigger costly KVCache
        recomputation, and introduce long-term compute and memory imbalance. We present RaidServe, a fault-tolerant
        TP serving system that sustains high performance under irregular GPU availability. RaidServe introduces three
        techniques to balance computation and memory across GPUs: (1) Cyclic KVCache Placement for even memory
        utilization, (2) Hybrid Attention combining tensor- and data-parallel attention to eliminate stragglers, and (3)
        Fine-Grained Load-Aware Routing to dynamically balance requests. It further employs proactive KVCache backup
        and on-demand weight recovery to avoid expensive recomputation and redundant data transfers. We implement
        these techniques in a lightweight serving engine compatible with existing LLM infrastructures. Evaluated on
        an 8×H100 DGX system with real-world fault traces and representative workloads, RaidServe achieves up to
        2× higher throughput and two orders of magnitude lower recovery latency compared to standard fault-handling
        approaches. Even with up to three GPU failures, RaidServe sustains high throughput and balanced utilization,
        demonstrating robust and efficient LLM serving under dynamic and unreliable hardware conditions.
