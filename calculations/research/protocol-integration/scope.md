# C68 protocol graph and early stream public integration

Two public commands integrate the frozen research work: `protocol-handshake` and `protocol-early-stream`. Both accept editable JSON through `--inputs`, output JSON or dedicated Markdown, and are included in unified reproduction. Seventeen handshake scenes and thirteen streaming scenes produce sixty fixed artifacts. The seven previously acquired official RFC originals are registered under `sources/protocol-rfc`; every calculation checks their hash and byte length with the common source loader. Their protocol constraints do not make declared packet lengths or processing times official measurements.

The handshake graph includes TCP+TLS1.3 and QUICv1 fresh/resumed/early-accepted/early-rejected/reused branches. Packet serialization, propagation, accepted request and complete response remain separate. Early sending permission, immediate versus wait-for-Finished execution policy, and application retry permission are independent. QUIC credit counts actually received UDP payload; a missing ACK/probe in a restricted graph is reported as restricted graph blockage, not a real protocol deadlock.

The streaming graph adds long early uploads whose key selection occurs at actual packet start. It preserves non-preemptive packets, key-installation priority at equal times, shared application packet numbers, STREAM offsets, accepted unique payload and authorized replay of the sent early prefix. The 30MB/5MB example now covers acceptance, authorized rejection retry and rejection without retry; a first response packet is not treated as a complete5MB response.

Public math is identical to each frozen candidate; only source provenance/root metadata changes. Thirty complete payload comparisons and sixty actual CLI JSON/Markdown comparisons passed before final unified artifact verification. Eight public tests preserve hand-derived times, QUIC credit and address-validation boundaries, mid-packet and same-time key transitions, offset multiplicities, packet numbers and invalid input rejection. Earlier independent research checks remain scoped evidence: handshake52 cases/21 rejected inputs and early-stream54 cases/15 rejected inputs.

This delivery does not implement HRR/Retry/invalid-ticket fallback, background tickets, full packet/TLS/HTTP encoding, general ACK/PTO/loss, actual congestion and flow control, or media deadlines. The reused handshake scene assumes an idle usable connection; existing finite connection-sequence credit is a separate teaching model, not an integrated real TCP/QUIC controller. C68 and the whole-book objective remain open. Final public test, reproducibility and outline results are saved in acceptance.json; a green verifier proves the registered scenarios, not complete book coverage.
