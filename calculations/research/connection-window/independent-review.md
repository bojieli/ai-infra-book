# Independent finite-window candidate review

No candidate correctness blocker found. `python calculations/research/connection-window/check-independent.py` passes83 identity/link replays:72 small-packet initial-window/receive-window/ACK-growth/upload-loss combinations,3 download-loss positions,6 published candidate scenarios, and2 controlled duplicate-event injections. Separate tests verify ACK/timer equality, illegal short-RTO rejection, and response initiation before upload's final ACK. Results are in check-independent.json. Candidate/public source was not edited.

The checker independently reconstructs received packet sets, contiguous prefixes and reorder occupancy, then replays sender state using only ACK arrivals. New packet admission must satisfy both unique outstanding credit and the right edge advertised by the sender's latest arrived prefix. Receiver knowledge is never used to grant early sender credit. Every window-event snapshot, cumulative ACK-credit increment, final cwnd, unique count, FIFO start/end and physical direction byte total is checked against the replay.

Important ownership behavior is correct: credit is reserved at admission into an irrevocable FIFO queue, not at later serialization start. The candidate explicitly declares this choice. Retransmission reuses an existing packet identity and reserves no additional unique credit, while paying new wire bytes. Duplicate ACKs release/grow zero; stale advertised prefixes cannot decrease the sender-visible maximum.

Duplicate data/ACK paths were actually exercised, despite the public scenario allowing only one dropped first transmission and no spontaneous duplicates. The checker temporarily wraps heap insertion within its own process, injecting one delayed duplicate data event or one delayed old-prefix ACK event. Candidate source is unchanged. The duplicate data produces one extra ACK and no extra unique delivery. Both injection cases preserve final cwnd and unique ACK counts, while late ACK arrival can extend protocol quiet time independently of all-unique-ACK completion.

Every data/ACK timer is checked at its originating serialization end plus declared RTO. The number of valid recovery timers and retransmission wire records is exactly0 or1 according to the configured loss. With data duration1s, propagation2s/3s and ACK serialization1s, ACK and timer meet at7s for RTO6s; the ACK cancels the timer, with no recovery. RTO5s rejects as outside the finite contract. This confirms event-priority behavior, without claiming adaptive TCP RTO or QUIC PTO implementation.

The model starts at complete input reception, not final upload ACK. In that exact-boundary scenario, response readiness occurs before the upload ACK reaches the client, but response serialization starts only after the same server→client serializer finishes that ACK. The checker reconstructs both shared direction clocks from all handshake/data/ACK reservations, preserving cross-transfer contention rather than resetting links between upload and download.

Final-image reception, all-unique-packets-ACKed, last true ACK/protocol quiet and future canceled timer events stay distinct. Quiet time equals the last actual ACK arrival; canceled timer heap entries do not extend it. No preview or measured-runtime claim is introduced.

Scope accepted for this finite candidate: selective-ID ACK with separately advertised contiguous-prefix flow control, declared per-ACK growth, fixed per-transmission timeout and one successful retry. There is no loss-window reset; the output states that departure explicitly. RFC6298 oldest-unacknowledged timer management/backoff, TCP fast recovery, QUIC ACK ranges/PTO, and actual HTTP/TLS handshake semantics are not inferred. Handshake entries remain explicit sequential teaching messages. This review does not close the rest of C68/C69.
